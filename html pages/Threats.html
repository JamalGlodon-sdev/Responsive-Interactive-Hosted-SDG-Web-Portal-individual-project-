What is harming life on land?

Forests are shrinking, more species are sliding towards extinction, and many of the most important wildlife sites are still unprotected. These are the main threats recorded in the UN's 2026 SDG 15 data.

Main Threats

Deforestation

The world has lost a net 203.1 million hectares of forest since 1990, including 41.0 million hectares since 2015. Latin America and the Caribbean lost 16.7% of its forest, and Sub-Saharan Africa and South-Eastern Asia each lost 14.9%.

Agricultural Expansion

Agriculture drives nearly 90% of global deforestation. Cropland expansion accounts for 49.6% of forest loss and livestock grazing for 38.5%. Oil palm alone was 7% of deforestation from 2000 to 2018.

Species Extinction

The Red List Index fell from over 0.80 in 1993 to below 0.75 in 2025. Warm-water reef-building corals are the worst affected: 44% of species now face a high risk of extinction, compared with about a third in 2008. Climate change, pollution, farm runoff, disease and unsustainable fishing all play a part.

Invasive Alien Species

A 2023 IPBES assessment found that invasive species cost the world more than $423 billion a year in 2019, with costs at least quadrupling every decade since 1970. Only 26% of countries have laws focused specifically on invasive species and biosecurity.

Protection Gaps

On average, 55% of each Key Biodiversity Area is still unprotected, and coverage in Oceania is below 25%. Countries are also likely missing about half of these sites, because they have not yet been fully identified.

Biggest Driver

Agricultural expansion causes nearly 90% of global deforestation.

Sources: UN SDG 15 goal page and the Extended Report 2026 (Goal 15).
