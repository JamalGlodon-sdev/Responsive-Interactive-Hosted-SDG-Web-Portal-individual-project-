What Is Working?

Progress is real but uneven. More land is protected, more forests have management plans, and more countries are building biodiversity into their policies and budgets.

Key Solutions

Protect Key Sites

Average protection of Key Biodiversity Areas rose from 25% in 2000 to 45% in 2025, and is above 60% in Northern America and Europe. New protected areas in 2025 include the Usala Gorilla Reserve (Democratic Republic of the Congo) and the Patagonia Azul Provincial Park (Argentina).

Manage Forests Sustainably

The share of forest with a long-term management plan rose from 54.4% in 2015 to 56.5% in 2025. In Eastern and South-Eastern Asia it nearly doubled, from 34.1% in 1990 to 64.7% in 2025. Certified forest reached 397 million hectares in 2024, and the yearly rate of forest loss is lower than in the 1990s.

Agree Global Rules

The Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework, adopted in 2022, sets 23 targets for 2030. 142 countries and the European Union have ratified the Nagoya Protocol on sharing the benefits of genetic resources, and more than 150 countries have set national targets to bring biodiversity into their policies and planning.

Count Nature in the Economy

By 2025, 98 countries were using the System of Environmental-Economic Accounting, up 42% since 2017, so ecosystems and biodiversity show up in national accounts.

Fund Biodiversity

Biodiversity-related development finance from OECD DAC members grew 42%, from USD 9.5 billion in 2015 to USD 13.5 billion in 2023. However, funding where biodiversity is the main goal fell 15% between 2015 and 2024.

Why 2030 Matters

Target 15.3 aims for a land degradation neutral world by 2030, and countries should update their Key Biodiversity Area networks by then.

Sources: UN SDG 15 goal page and the Extended Report 2026 (Goal 15).
